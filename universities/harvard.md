## 2. Institutional identity — who they think they are

- **Veritas is load-bearing, not branding.** Harvard's oldest self-description is a single word about truth-seeking; the College's mission language is "citizens and citizen-leaders for our society." The pairing matters: intellect *in service of society* is the house style. An applicant whose stated ambition is to build the legal architecture for an AI-transformed society is speaking Harvard's native dialect.
- **"The best educators of one another."** The admissions office's own "What We Look For" page says it seeks students "who will be the best educators of one another and their professors — individuals who will inspire those around them." Harvard is explicitly composing a residential community, not ranking résumés. The roommate and contribution prompts are direct instruments of that.
- **Continuity at the top of admissions.** Dean William Fitzsimmons '67 is in his 39th year leading admissions as of 2026 (no successor announced as of April 2026 reporting) — the whole-person, "expanded view of excellence" doctrine he built is stable. President Alan Garber's public posture through 2025–26 (amid federal pressure) has doubled down on institutional independence and academic excellence — themes of intellectual integrity and truth under pressure are unusually salient at Harvard right now, which quietly flatters a "disagreement" essay done well.
- **Anti-burnout messaging is sincere.** Harvard's own application pages warn against "more is always better" AP-stacking and cite their "Time Out or Burn Out" essay. They say they'd rather see depth, reading, and research on subjects of great interest. This is institutional cover for a super-curricular framing: one deep instrument (violin) plus one deep intellectual project beats a padded activities list.

## 3. By the numbers — what the CDS says

**C7 factor ratings — verified from the latest CDS by Henry, Aug 2026 (authoritative; do not trust older third-party summaries that show "Very Important" rows):**

| Rating | Factors |
|---|---|
| Very Important | **None** |
| Important | **None** |
| Considered | Rigor, GPA, test scores, essay, recommendations, interview, extracurriculars, talent/ability, character/personal qualities, first-generation, alumni relation, geographical residence, volunteer work, work experience |
| Not Considered | Class rank, state residency, religious affiliation, **level of applicant's interest** |

**Decoding a pure no-hierarchy C7.** Harvard is nearly unique in refusing to rank any factor above any other. Read this literally and it says four strategic things:

1. **There is no lever to pull — only a gestalt to compose.** No single element (scores, an award, the supplement) can carry the file, and no single weakness sinks it. The file is read as a *person*; the win condition is coherence — every component pointing at the same recognizable human. For candidate that means the SAT, the violin supplement, the essays, and the recommenders must all describe the same mind: pattern-seeking, verbal, disciplined, aimed at law-for-the-AI-age.
2. **Talent/ability is "Considered" — so the arts supplement is a real but bounded asset.** At schools that rate talent "Very Important," a supplement can be decisive; at Harvard it is one more brushstroke. Submit the violin recording if it is genuinely strong (Harvard's own language: supplements help when they "reveal unusual talent"), but do not build the application's case on it.
3. **Demonstrated interest is officially worthless.** Level of interest: Not Considered. Campus visits, info-session sign-ins, and contact with admissions buy nothing. The REA advantage (8.74% vs 2.77% last published) is mostly pool composition (recruited athletes, strongest-prepared applicants), not an interest reward — but applying REA still puts the file in a smaller, earlier-read pool.
4. **Contrast with peers.** MIT's latest CDS marks exactly one factor Very Important (character/personal qualities); most elites rank rigor/GPA at the top. Harvard alone declines to declare. Practically: essays and recommendations do proportionally more work here than the C7 admits, because they are what turns fourteen "Considered" data points into one person.

**Scores in context.** candidate's 1510 superscore sits exactly at the enrolled 25th percentile (1510–1580). Math 790 **equals the enrolled median (790)** — genuinely strong. EBRW 720 is **20 points below the enrolled 25th percentile (740)**. Because Harvard *requires* scores, there is no submit/withhold decision to make — the only question is whether to retake. See Honest Flags.

## 4. The application surface

**Cycle status: VERIFIED LIVE.** The five short-answer prompts below were pulled from Harvard's official Application Requirements page on Aug 8, 2026 (page footer © 2026; portal references "Class of 2030" admits, i.e., the current cycle). **The 2026–27 set is unchanged from 2025–26.** Third-party guides (Ivy Coach, College Essay Advisors) concur. These are the prompts to draft against.

**Personal statement:** Common App essay (650 words), standard prompts, shared across schools.

**Harvard supplement — five required short answers, 150-word limit each** (verbatim, official 2026–27):

1. "Harvard has long recognized the importance of enrolling a student body with a diversity of perspectives and experiences. How will the life experiences that shaped who you are today enable you to contribute to Harvard?"
2. "Describe a time when you strongly disagreed with someone about an idea or issue. How did you communicate or engage with this person? What did you learn from this experience?"
3. "Briefly describe any of your extracurricular activities, employment experience, travel, or family responsibilities that have shaped who you are."
4. "How do you hope to use your Harvard education in the future?"
5. "Top 3 things your roommates might like to know about you."

**SFFA context on prompt 1.** This five-question set was introduced in 2023–24, immediately after *SFFA v. Harvard*, replacing the old optional long essay. Prompt 1 is Harvard's implementation of the Supreme Court's carve-out — the Court barred race as a category but expressly allowed "an applicant's discussion of how race affected his or her life, be it through discrimination, inspiration, or otherwise," tied to individual qualities. Translation: identity and background material lands here *only* as lived experience that produced a demonstrable capacity to contribute. Write the experience and its output, never the category.

**Logistics:**
- REA Nov 1 / RD Jan 1. Counselors get one extra week for school materials. Midyear report not required at REA deadline (send in February if deferred).
- Two teacher evaluations (different academic subjects) + Secondary School Report with counselor letter. Additional recommenders possible post-submission via portal link.
- REA testing: results wanted by end of October; November SAT sitting accepted. RD accepts through February sittings.
- **REA restriction rules (official):** may not simultaneously apply EA/ED/REA to any other *private* U.S. institution, nor to a binding public program. **Allowed:** non-binding EA at public universities, service academies, international universities, rolling admission, and early scholarship/program deadlines elsewhere if non-binding and timing-required. REA is non-binding — full RD shopping and aid comparison preserved. *List-strategy consequence: Harvard REA forecloses MIT EA, Yale/Princeton/Stanford REA — pick one.*
- **Arts supplement:** optional media (video/audio/images) via **SlideRoom** (harvard.slideroom.com); small fee, waivable. Evaluated by faculty *at the Admissions Committee's discretion*. Academic work (research papers, writing) goes through the Applicant Portal upload instead — a second, underused supplement channel that fits a CogSci/law-flavored research paper if one exists. Backup: YouTube links via portal if SlideRoom fails. No published separate arts deadline — submit close to the application deadline; confirm exact SlideRoom cutoffs when the portal opens.
- QuestBridge partner; fee waivers routine.

## 5. Programs & assets mapped to candidate

**The CogSci/SymSys analog — honest read first:** Harvard has **no standalone Cognitive Science or Symbolic Systems concentration.** What it has instead is arguably richer but structurally different: the **Mind Brain Behavior (MBB) Interfaculty Initiative** (founded 1993), an honors-track overlay available *within* seven concentrations — psychology, philosophy, computer science, linguistics, neuroscience, history & science, human evolutionary biology. MBB students take Neuro 80 (Neurobiology of Behavior), a junior interdisciplinary MBB seminar, and write an MBB-approved senior thesis. So the Stanford-SymSys question "what is a mind?" is asked at Harvard *from inside a home department*.

- **Psychology concentration → Cognitive Science (MBB) track.** The named track literally called "Cognitive Science" — mechanisms of memory, perception, language, cognition — with less bio coursework than the sibling **Cognitive Neuroscience & Evolutionary Psychology (CNEP) life-sciences track**. Either is a direct fit; the Cognitive Science (MBB) track is the closest single thing Harvard has to SymSys.
- **Philosophy concentration → MBB track** — the mind/language/logic route, and the most natural on-ramp to law's analytical core.
- **Government concentration → Tech Science program.** A named optional track inside Government, chaired by **Latanya Sweeney** (Daniel Paul Professor of the Practice of Government and Technology; founder of the Data Privacy Lab; famous for re-identification research that shaped privacy law). Requirements include "Foundations of Technology" courses and **Gov 93B, the Tech Science capstone practicum**, where students attack a live technology–society conflict. Sweeney's stated goal: train "technologists" for public service and governance of technology. *This is the single most on-thesis undergraduate program Harvard offers for an IP/AI/robotics-law trajectory.*
- **Economics concentration** — full-strength option (her #2 interest); law & economics exposure feeds antitrust/IP economics.
- **Embedded EthiCS** (Philosophy × SEAS, since 2017): philosophy PhDs co-teach ethics modules inside a dozen-plus CS courses (intro programming through AI — e.g., "machines and moral decision making" in Intro to AI). She can take CS courses that come pre-wired with the ethics-of-computing questions a technology lawyer lives on, without concentrating in CS.
- **Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society** — Harvard Law School's internet/AI-policy powerhouse. Undergraduates are eligible: academic-year **research assistant** positions (recent calls: machine learning and AI workstreams) and the paid **summer internship ("Berktern") program** (10 weeks, ~$21/hr; Summer 2026 apps closed March 22 — expect similar timing annually). Current initiatives include **AI and Youth Safety**. For the "future use of a Harvard education" answer, Berkman Klein is the proper noun that proves she has mapped the terrain from College to Law School.
- **Pre-law pipeline:** No pre-law major (by design — law schools prefer depth). Advising runs through the **Mignone Center for Career Success** (law-school guide, Law School Night, workshops on **Harvard Law's Junior Deferral Program** — HLS admits Harvard College juniors who defer two years) plus **pre-law tutors embedded in every upperclass House**. Caveat: a Jan 2026 Crimson op-ed argues centralized pre-law advising is thinner than peer schools' — the real pre-law asset is proximity (HLS is across the street, cross-registration exists for advanced undergrads) rather than hand-holding.
- **Violin as super-curricular:** **Harvard-Radcliffe Orchestra** (Pierian Sodality, 1808 — oldest symphonic orchestra in America by its own count; 2026–27 auditions Aug 28–31, two contrasting pieces) — a serious, auditioned, non-conservatory home. Office for the Arts lessons/ensembles; Music Department open to non-concentrators; a possible Music secondary (minor). **Harvard/NEC footnote (retired thesis):** the dual AB+MM path still exists (supplement asks the NEC question directly; ~5 students/yr). Answer "No" unless the family deliberately revives it — checking Yes reframes the whole file around conservatory intent, which contradicts the Einstein-model positioning.
- **Concentration choice mechanics:** Harvard admits to the College, not to a concentration (declared sophomore fall); the supplement asks for top-3 fields of interest. Suggested slate consistent with the thesis: **Government + Psychology + Philosophy** (or Economics third) — signals the CogSci-to-law arc without gaming.

## 6. What the reader rewards (analyst's model)

Harvard's reader is composing a 1,600-person residential college, one seat at a time, with no scoring rubric they'll admit to. The C7 refusal to rank factors is the tell: the committee's operative question is not "how strong is this file?" but "**who is this person, and what do they do to a dinner table conversation?**" The mission language (citizen-leaders), the "best educators of one another" doctrine, and the five community-probing short answers all point the same way: Harvard rewards a *legible, coherent identity with civic torque* — a student whose intellect has a direction, whose direction serves society, and whose presence teaches others. It distrusts padding (its own site warns against course-stacking) and ignores flattery (interest: Not Considered). Post-SFFA, it reads for lived experience converted into contributive capacity. The winning file here is not the most decorated one; it is the one where fourteen "Considered" fragments snap into a single memorable human — ideally one Harvard can imagine, decades on, standing between society and its most dangerous new machines.

## 8. Ranked essay angles

1. **"The lawyer the machines will need" — thesis angle.** *One sentence:* She is preparing to be the technology lawyer who understands minds — human and artificial — well enough to write the rules between them. *Evidence:* interest ranking (lawyer > economist > psychologist > applied math), M790 quant grounding, CogSci orientation. *Lands in:* **Prompt 4 (future use of a Harvard education)** — name Government's **Tech Science track and Gov 93B capstone under Latanya Sweeney**, the **Cognitive Science (MBB) track in Psychology**, a **Berkman Klein** RA/Berktern ambition, and HLS's Junior Deferral Program as the visible arc. Specific proper nouns are the whole game in 150 words.
2. **"Violin is how I think" — the Einstein angle.** *One sentence:* A decade of violin taught her the disciplines a good lawyer-scientist runs on — hearing structure inside noise, practicing until precision becomes instinct, performing under stakes. *Evidence:* years of serious study; supplement recording substantiates. *Lands in:* **Prompt 3 (activities that shaped you)**, with the HRO audition (Aug 28–31 window — she'd arrive audition-ready) as the forward hook; echo lightly in Prompt 5. Explicitly *not* a career claim — that's what makes it fresh at a school drowning in conservatory-track violinists.
3. **"Disagreement as cross-examination practice" — the veritas angle.** *One sentence:* A real disagreement she navigated by steel-manning the other side — showing the exact temperament of a future advocate and of Harvard's truth-under-pressure moment. *Evidence:* needs a true story from her life (debate, family, ensemble section dispute over interpretation — an HRO-style musical disagreement would fuse angles 2 and 3). *Lands in:* **Prompt 2 (disagreement)**. Given Garber-era Harvard's public identity fight, intellectual honesty over conflict-avoidance will read as unusually on-brand.
4. **"What I bring to the table" — contribution angle.** *One sentence:* The specific life experiences (family, culture, community — whatever is true and formative) that built a bridge-person between quantitative and humanistic worlds, and how that shows up in rooms she's in. *Evidence:* to be drawn from profile intake §6 (currently empty — priority interview material). *Lands in:* **Prompt 1 (life experiences → contribution)**. Post-SFFA rules: experience → quality → contribution, never category alone.
5. **"Three true things" — roommate angle.** *One sentence:* Three concrete, humanizing specifics (e.g., practices scales before breakfast; will argue either side of any question for sport; a genuinely odd habit or love) that make the gestalt likable. *Lands in:* **Prompt 5.** Its job is warmth — the other four carry the thesis; this one proves she's good company, which at "best educators of one another" Harvard is not a throwaway.

## 9. Honest flags

- **This is a lottery-grade reach for everyone.** 4.2% overall; sub-3% in RD. No fit analysis changes that. Harvard belongs on the list only atop a sound target/safety base.
- **EBRW 720 is the one measurable soft spot — and it points at her stated profession.** Enrolled EBRW mid-50% is 740–780; she's 20 below the floor, while claiming a *verbal* career (law). Scores must be submitted (requirement — no strategy choice exists), so the only decision is a retake. Recommendation: **one EBRW-focused retake targeting 750+**, Aug/Sep/Oct sitting (REA accepts through the November SAT). M790 needs no defense — it equals the enrolled median. If the retake doesn't happen or doesn't move, the essays and English/history teacher rec have to carry the verbal case instead; that's workable but thinner.
- **No Cognitive Science concentration exists.** Do not write "I want to study cognitive science at Harvard" — a reader knows there's no such concentration. Write the Harvard-native version: Psychology's Cognitive Science (MBB) track, or Government + MBB coursework. Getting this vocabulary right is itself a fit signal; getting it wrong is a tell.
- **MBB is an overlay, not a major; Tech Science is optional within Government; Berkman Klein is HLS-centered** (undergrad access is real — RA posts, Berkterns — but competitive and mostly summer). Don't overstate any of them as guaranteed undergraduate homes.
- **Pre-law advising is decentralized and, per a Jan 2026 Crimson critique, thinner than peers'.** The honest pitch is proximity and ecosystem (HLS across the street, JDP, Berkman Klein), not concierge advising.
- **The middle of the file is currently empty.** Activities, leadership, awards, GPA/rigor are not in the intake. Under pure gestalt review an application is exactly as strong as its weakest coherent part — this is the most urgent data gap, ahead of any essay work.
- **Arts supplement expectations:** Harvard states most admitted students submit none and they help only when revealing "unusual talent." Submit only a recording that a conservatory-adjacent listener would call excellent; a merely-good one dilutes the gestalt. (Repertoire/level not yet on file — decision pending real evidence.)
- **The retired NEC framing still lives in older folder files** (1-Pager "Fit" section). Treat this report as superseding; answer the supplement's NEC question "No" absent a deliberate reversal.

## 10. Strategic levers & action checklist

**Levers**
- **REA (Nov 1) is the main structural lever.** Non-binding, aid-safe (need-blind + the $100K/$200K initiative removes any ED-style aid-leverage worry), and the early pool historically admits at ~3x the RD rate even net of hooked applicants. Cost: forecloses MIT EA and Yale/Princeton/Stanford REA — a one-slot decision to make across the whole list by mid-October.
- **Demonstrated interest is dead — reallocate that time.** No visits-for-show, no mailing-list theater. Hours go to the EBRW retake, the recording, and the essays.
- **Two supplement channels, not one:** SlideRoom (violin) *and* the Applicant Portal academic upload (a research paper or serious written work on an AI/law/cognition question, if one exists or can be finished by fall). For a gestalt reader, a genuine written artifact does for the "lawyer" claim what the recording does for the violin claim.
- **Financial aid is a pure tailwind:** depending on family income, Harvard is free or tuition-free — likely the cheapest option on the entire list if admitted. No aid-driven reason to hesitate on REA.

**Checklist (2026–27)**
- [ ] Decide the single early slot (Harvard REA vs. MIT EA vs. other REA) — by early October.
- [ ] Register for Aug/Sep/Oct SAT; EBRW-targeted prep to 750+ (Nov sitting = last REA-usable).
- [ ] Fill the intake's empty sections (activities, honors, GPA/rigor, story material) — prerequisite for essays.
- [ ] Select and record two contrasting violin pieces (doubles as HRO-audition prep evidence); SlideRoom submission near app deadline; request fee waiver.
- [ ] Draft the five 150-word answers against §8 (prompts verified live for 2026–27 — no re-verification needed).
- [ ] Recommenders: one quantitative (math/econ) + one humanities (English/history — carries the verbal case); counselor briefed on the CogSci→law thesis so the school report tells the same story.
- [ ] Supplement form: fields of study Government / Psychology / Philosophy-or-Economics; NEC question = No; activities: Orchestra.
