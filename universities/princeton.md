## 2. Institutional identity — who they think they are

- **Service is the brand, and it is load-bearing, not marketing.** The informal motto appears in commencement speeches, the Pace Center for Civic Engagement's programming ("Be In the Service of Humanity"), and President Eisgruber's own framing: in revising the motto he said Princeton must "serve not only our state or our tribe but all members of the human community." A dedicated supplemental essay (250 words) exists solely to probe this. No other Ivy ties an essay this explicitly to its motto.
- **Undergraduate teaching + independent scholarship.** Princeton is the Ivy that markets itself as the one that never de-prioritized undergrads: no law/med/business schools competing for attention, an 8:1 ratio, precepts, and the universal senior thesis. It sees itself as a place where every student becomes a producer of scholarship, not a consumer.
- **Liberal-arts breadth with research depth.** The A.B. academic essay prompt is literally about exploring "across the humanities and the arts, the natural sciences, and the social sciences." Princeton rewards students who connect fields, not just excel in one.
- **Tone calibration:** earnest, humble, civically serious. Princeton's register is closer to "quiet purpose" than to swagger (Harvard) or whimsy (UChicago). "In the Nation's Service" should be *echoed in substance* — how her work serves others — never quoted back as a slogan.

**What the decode means for candidate:**
1. **Test scores are rated "Very Important" even while optional** — and Princeton is ending test-optional next cycle precisely because its internal five-year review found submitters outperformed non-submitters. Princeton is telling you it believes in scores. (See §9–10 for the submit call.)
2. **Talent/ability: Very Important** → the violin arts supplement carries real, rated weight. It is reviewed by music faculty only and feeds back into the admission file.
3. **Essays + recommendations + character in the top tier, with nothing in the middle tier** → the written record (essays, graded paper, letters) is where files are separated. For a verbally-positioned future lawyer, this is the terrain to win on.
4. **Demonstrated interest: Not Considered** → campus visits, email-opens, etc. move nothing. Do the cheap hygiene, spend the saved hours on essays.
5. Older CDS years (2021–24) show materially the same top tier; the notable evolution is test scores sitting firmly in "Very Important." Flag: exact year-by-year C7 wording — verify against official PDFs.

### Cost & aid (2026–27 published; policy figures from Princeton announcements + CDS secondary)
- Sticker COA ~$95,600 (tuition $68,140 + fees/room/board/expenses)
- **Need-blind for ALL applicants including international; 100% of need met; no-loan since 2001** (first in the nation)
- Families **up to $100,000: pay nothing** (announced 2022). A **2025 expansion** extends full-cost-of-attendance coverage to **most families earning up to ~$150,000**, with sharply reduced cost above that (one report: $150k income ≈ $12,500/yr, down from ~$23,675). Average need-based grant ~$79,320 (CDS 2025–26). ~11% of recent grads borrowed at all. **Verify exact current tiers on Princeton's aid site / net price calculator — run the NPC.**
- Bottom line for a family needing aid: Princeton is arguably the **single strongest aid program in the country**, and SCEA is non-binding, so applying early carries zero aid risk — you can compare offers until May 1.

### Required components
1. **Common App (or QuestBridge)** with 650-word personal statement
2. **Princeton academic essay** — A.B. version for candidate (250 words max):
   > "As a research institution that also prides itself on its liberal arts curriculum, Princeton allows students to explore areas across the humanities and the arts, the natural sciences, and the social sciences. What academic areas most pique your curiosity, and how do the programs offered at Princeton suit your particular interests?"
3. **Your Voice #1 — lived experience** (500 words max):
   > "Princeton values community and encourages students, faculty, staff and leadership to engage in respectful conversations that can expand their perspectives and challenge their ideas and beliefs. As a prospective member of this community, reflect on how your lived experiences will impact the conversations you will have in the classroom, the dining hall or other campus spaces. What lessons have you learned in life thus far? What will your classmates learn from you? In short, how has your lived experience shaped you?"
4. **Your Voice #2 — service & civic engagement** (250 words max):
   > "Princeton has a longstanding commitment to understanding our responsibility to society through service and civic engagement. How does your own story intersect with these ideals?"
5. **More About You** — all three, ≤50 words each ("There are no right or wrong answers. Be yourself!"):
   - "What is a new skill you would like to learn in college?"
   - "What brings you joy?"
   - "What song represents the soundtrack of your life at this moment?"
6. **Graded written paper (required):** expository writing from an academic course (**preferably English, history, or social studies**) from the last three years of high school incl. senior year; **1–2 pages is sufficient**; must show the **teacher's grade and comments** (include rubric if used). No creative writing. Submitted with the app or via the portal.
7. **Recommendations:** counselor + **two teacher letters**; school report; transcript.
8. **Testing:** optional this cycle (see §1). Princeton **superscores** — highest section across dates (same test format only; no mixing paper/digital).
9. **Interview:** optional alumni conversation where available; C7 rates it merely "Considered."

### Optional Arts Supplement (music) — verified on official page, Aug 2026
- **Indicate intent in Princeton's Common App member questions**; submit via the **Princeton Applicant Status Portal** *after* submitting the application.
- **Deadlines: Nov 6 (SCEA) / Jan 8 (RD)**, 11:59 p.m. applicant's local time. No edits after submission; one submission per program.
- **Classical Instrument requirements:** **10–15 minutes of advanced solo work showing contrasting examples of expression and technique.** No orchestral/ensemble footage unless prominently featured solo.
- Must attach a **music résumé** (separate media upload) and a **personal statement (≤250 words) on how she would integrate music-making and ensemble contribution into her Princeton studies** — treat this as a real essay, it goes to the music faculty reviewer.
- Optional arts letter of recommendation — must be requested *before* submission (and requires the résumé upload).
- Reviewed **solely by arts faculty**; contact for music supplement questions: Rob George (rg8407@princeton.edu).
- Formats: standard audio/video files up to 5GB, or YouTube/Vimeo/SoundCloud links (no personal websites).

## 5. Programs & assets mapped to candidate

### The CogSci → law stack (her thesis, assembled Princeton-style)
Princeton has **no Cognitive Science major** — confirmed. What it has is a *buildable* stack that maps almost one-to-one onto "CogSci-driven technology lawyer":

- **Minor in Cognitive Science** (cogsci.princeton.edu) — confirmed current: 5 courses, ≥3 at 300-level, spanning ≥3 academic units; enroll from spring of sophomore year after declaring a major. This is Princeton's SymSys-adjacent offering: philosophy of mind, psych, neuro, linguistics, computation.
- **CITP — Center for Information Technology Policy** (joint venture of SPIA, CS, and Engineering): the tech-policy crown jewel. **Key new fact: starting Fall 2025, CITP offers a *Minor in Computing, Technology and Policy* open to students graduating Spring 2027 and beyond** — i.e., fully available to candidate's class. (It replaces the old Technology & Society / IT-track certificate, which sunsets after Spring 2026.) Independent work presented at an annual spring symposium. This is arguably the single most on-thesis undergraduate credential at any Ivy for AI/tech law.
- **Law@Princeton** (SPIA + University Center for Human Values): umbrella for the **Program in Law and Public Policy (P•LAW)** and UCHV's **Program in Law and Normative Thinking**. Princeton has no law school, but it has a deep jurisprudence tradition (the McCormick Professorship of Jurisprudence dates to 1896; Corwin's constitutional scholarship; today Robert George — verify current holder).
- **Pre-law advising:** dedicated adviser (Brandon Treviño) in the Center for Career Development, published pre-law guide, LSAT resources, law-school application support. Princeton is a perennial top feeder to T14 law schools (no invented numbers — placement stats not verified this session).

### Candidate majors (A.B.; major declared end of sophomore year — she does not have to choose now, and the academic essay can honestly present the stack rather than one department)
- **Psychology** — elite department; natural home for the CogSci minor; Princeton Neuroscience Institute adjacent (Neuroscience is a separate major and minor).
- **SPIA (School of Public and International Affairs)** — open-enrollment major; policy + institutions; hosts CITP and Law@Princeton; the most direct "law track" home.
- **Politics** — public law and political theory strength; jurisprudence tradition lives here too.
- **Economics** — elite; behavioral economics legacy (Kahneman's home for decades); the **Kahneman-Treisman Center for Behavioral Science & Public Policy** links psych → econ → policy (verify current center name/programming).
- **Applied math:** the **Program in Applied & Computational Mathematics (PACM) minor** serves the quant interest without requiring the math major.

### The written-scholar infrastructure (her lawyer muscles)
- **Universal junior independent work + senior thesis** — every A.B. student writes a thesis; a future litigator/scholar can write hers on, e.g., cognitive science and AI liability doctrine, supervised across SPIA/Psych/CITP.
- **The required graded written paper** is a structural gift to a verbally-positioned applicant: it is the one component where she can *show* analytic writing with a teacher's independent validation — partially offsetting the EBRW 720 (see §9).
- Precepts (discussion seminars) reward exactly the "respectful conversations" the 500-word essay probes.

### Music (super-curricular framing — correct fit at Princeton)
- **Minor in Music Performance (MPP)** — Princeton converted its Performance certificate into this minor: rigorous, **by competitive audition, open to rising juniors majoring outside music**. Also a general **Music minor**. Note the older 1-Pager's "Performance certificate" language is outdated — it's a minor now.
- **Princeton University Orchestra** (flagship), rich chamber music, faculty-led ensembles, Richardson Auditorium.
- **No conservatory dual degree** — under the retired BA+MM framing this was a deal-breaker; under the super-curricular framing it is **irrelevant, and Princeton becomes one of the best-aligned schools on the list for the Einstein model**: serious performance inside a liberal-arts identity, credentialed by a minor, showcased by the arts supplement, with zero pressure to professionalize.
- Caution for essays: the MPP minor is audition-gated and junior-year entry — write "I will audition for the Music Performance minor," not "I will do it."

## 6. What the reader rewards (analyst's model)

A Princeton reader is scanning ~42,000 files for three signals stacked in order. First, **academic near-perfection as table stakes** — rigor, ~4.0, and (from files that have them) scores in range; this keeps you alive but distinguishes nothing, since 72% of the class had a 4.0. Second, **a rated, verifiable distinctive talent** — C7's "talent/ability: Very Important" plus a faculty-reviewed arts supplement channel means Princeton institutionally *prices* excellence like conservatory-level violin, rather than treating it as garnish. Third — and this is where Princeton diverges from Harvard's power-seeking and Yale's community-warmth registers — **purpose pointed outward**. The motto essay, the Pace Center, Eisgruber's "all members of the human community": the reader is asking *whom will this brilliance serve?* The winning file reads as: a scholar who will write a serious thesis, a talent the faculty vouches for, and a life trajectory whose beneficiary is not primarily the applicant. Humility of tone matters; Princeton's own prompt coaching is literally "Be yourself." The essay set is long (250 + 500 + 250 + 3×50 + graded paper) — Princeton deliberately over-samples *writing*, which is exactly the surface a future lawyer should dominate.

## 8. Ranked essay angles

1. **"The mind is the missing witness" — academic essay (250w).** Angle: AI law is being written by people who understand statutes or code, rarely cognition; she wants to study *minds* rigorously so the law of intelligent machines is grounded in the science of intelligence. Evidence: lawyer>economist>psychologist ranking, M790 quant credibility, CogSci orientation. Anchors: **CogSci minor + the new CITP Minor in Computing, Technology and Policy + Law@Princeton/P•LAW + senior thesis** (name a thesis-shaped question, e.g., what cognitive science says about "intent" for autonomous systems). This prompt exists for exactly this kind of cross-listed ambition — answer it with the stack, not one department.
2. **"In the service of a society that thinks with machines" — service essay (250w).** Angle: her civic claim is *forward-looking service*: an AI-transformed society will need advocates who protect human agency (IP, robotics liability, algorithmic accountability). Echo the motto's substance — nation *and* humanity — without quoting it. **Provisional:** this essay needs one concrete already-done service/advocacy datum to avoid reading as aspiration-only; pull from the activities list once on file.
3. **"Practice room as thinking room" — lived-experience essay (500w).** Angle: the Einstein model made explicit — a decade of violin as her epistemology: how score-study, intonation feedback loops, and ensemble listening trained the habits (patience, error-correction, hearing others precisely) she'll bring to precepts and dining-hall arguments. Directly answers "what will your classmates learn from you?" Pairs with (and is corroborated by) the arts supplement — essay claims, faculty-reviewed recording proves.
4. **Soundtrack short answer (50w).** The gift question for a violinist: pick a specific work she actually plays, tie it to this moment in one turn of phrase. Low-effort, high-charm; also quietly reinforces the supplement.
5. **Graded written paper as positioning artifact.** Not an essay, but choose it like one: her strongest *analytic* English/history/social-studies paper (argument, evidence, counterargument), teacher grade + comments visible, 1–2 pages. This is the counter-exhibit to EBRW 720 — it shows the lawyer's actual product. If a current-year paper on a law/tech/mind-adjacent topic exists or can be produced this fall, prefer it.

Coordination note: personal statement (650w) should carry the *person* (formation story), keeping the Princeton academic essay free to be densely specific about the stack — avoid double-spending the violin material across both long essays.

## 9. Honest flags

1. **4.4% admit rate; ~2.3% if international.** Nothing in this report changes that Princeton is a hard reach for everyone; with GPA/activities unverified, even "competitive" is provisional. (It is correctly filed in Reach tier.)
2. **EBRW 720 is below the enrolled 25th percentile (740) — on her positioning axis.** A "future lawyer" with a sub-p25 verbal score is a visible tension in a file read by C7-driven readers who rate test scores Very Important. The composite 1510 *is* in range (1490–1560), and Princeton superscores, but this is the profile's one soft number. **The EBRW retake to 750+ is the highest-ROI move available before November.**
3. **No Cognitive Science major.** Her intellectual home exists at Princeton only as a *minor plus an assembled stack*. That's honest and workable (and the thesis culture arguably makes it richer than a packaged major), but Stanford SymSys or a true CogSci major elsewhere is a cleaner named home. Essays must show she's mapped the assembly — vagueness here reads as not having done the homework.
4. **Music credential is deferred and gated:** the Music Performance minor is by competitive audition, junior-year entry. No conservatory dual degree (irrelevant under current framing, but the older 1-Pager/intake language must not leak into essays).
5. **Service evidence gap (provisional):** Princeton has a dedicated service essay and a service identity; the file currently contains no documented service/civic record. If one exists, get it into the intake; if thin, the essay must be honest about scale.
6. **SCEA is mutually exclusive with every other private early program** — including MIT EA and Harvard/Yale REA. Choosing Princeton SCEA is choosing it as the single early bet (see §10).
